Recycled Polymer Feedstock for Large-Format Additive Manufacturing

ABB IRB 1600 Rapid Fusion Thermo-Prism Creaform HandySCAN Recycled Polypropylene

Project Overview

This research investigated the feasibility of recycled polypropylene as a feedstock for Large-Format Additive Manufacturing (LFAM). The project integrated recycled material preparation, extrusion calibration, robotic deposition and 3D metrology to evaluate manufacturing performance and dimensional accuracy.

01

Feedstock Preparation

Recycled polypropylene material was processed through granulation to produce feedstock suitable for pellet-based extrusion.

02

Extrusion Calibration

Controlled material-discharge measurements were used to characterise extrusion behaviour and support calibration of the deposition process.

03

Robotic Manufacturing

Components were manufactured using a six-axis ABB IRB 1600 industrial robot integrated with a Rapid Fusion Thermo-Prism twin-screw extrusion system.

04

3D Scanning & Validation

Manufactured geometry was captured using a Creaform HandySCAN. Multiple scans were aligned and merged before comparison with nominal CAD geometry to evaluate dimensional variation. These maps show geometric conformity after registration; they do not measure adhesion strength.

Engineering Focus

The project combines circular polymer processing, large-format additive manufacturing, industrial robotics and digital metrology to investigate how recycled material behaviour influences manufacturing accuracy and process repeatability.

Aerospace Seat Bracket Optimisation

Additive Manufacturing & Generative Design

The aerospace industry requires lightweight, high-complexity components to reduce fuel consumption and emissions. This project sought to replace a traditional, overengineered solid aluminium seat bracket (163.85 g) with an optimised 3D-printed alternative.

01 Material Evaluation

Conducted tensile and flexural testing comparing FDM (Markforged Onyx), SLS (Nylon PA12) and Material Jetting (J850 Tough). Markforged Onyx produced an ultimate tensile strength of 104.02 MPa and a flexural modulus of 5.89 GPa in the project test programme.

02 Generative Setup & FEA

Used Autodesk Fusion 360 to define preserve geometries around the mounting interfaces and obstacle regions around the surrounding assembly. A safety factor constraint of 2.0 was applied with load cases representing upright (150 N) and reclined (200 N) passenger positions, followed by static stress validation of the generated form.

03 Results & Sustainability

The final printed Markforged Onyx component weighed 12.3 g, representing a 92.5% mass reduction compared with the original 163.85 g aluminium component. A Granta Eco Audit projected a 99.2% reduction in lifecycle carbon emissions over a 20-year aircraft lifespan, while physical testing produced a safety factor of 3.62.

Bespoke VR Headset Architecture

3D Scanning & Human-Centred Design

Standard VR head straps can be poorly suited to users with high-volume afro-textured hair, creating discomfort and hair compression. This project explored a bespoke, collision-aware headset architecture tailored around a Pixel 8 Pro and scan-derived user geometry.

01 Dual-Modality Scanning

Used an Artec Leo to capture rigid facial and forehead geometry for contact-pad development, while Kiri Engine photogrammetry was used to capture the volumetric boundary of the hair. The datasets were aligned in Fusion 360 to create a scan-derived model for collision-aware design development.

02 Spatial Ideation & DFA

Initial concepting and spatial layout were developed in Gravity Sketch before moving into Fusion 360 for detailed engineering. A 15 mm offset was introduced between the user's eyes and the lenses, together with Design for Assembly features for securely locating the mobile device.

03 Generative Design

Preserve regions were defined around the lens frames, contact pads and phone mounts, while the face, visual path and hair volume were treated as obstacle geometry. A 5 N downward load was applied to guide the Fusion 360 generative design study toward a lightweight structure that avoided the scanned hair volume.

Bike Curious: L3E Urban Mobility

Rapid Prototyping & Iterative Design

Bike Curious is an L3E-class electric moped concept developed around a short urban commute. The project focused on iterative digital design, troubleshooting and rapid physical prototyping, with sustainability explored through material and component choices.

01 Visual Ideation & CAD

Initial visual concepts were explored in Vizcom before a selected side profile was imported into Autodesk Fusion as a reference canvas. The digital model was then developed using form tools, splines and extruded features, including a custom battery casing and detailed wheel geometry.

02 Prototyping Failures & Iteration

Early FDM prototypes exposed practical manufacturing issues including poor first-layer setup, warping, layer shifts and fragile handlebars. These failures were used as design inputs, driving changes to geometry, print orientation and component robustness before the next iteration.

03 Print Optimisation & Assembly

To improve printability, the CAD model was split into two halves joined by internal pins and mating holes, reducing the need for major support structures. The final prototype used a 0.6 mm nozzle and 0.3 mm layer height, with ironing and external perimeter settings refined to improve surface finish and reduce unnecessary production time.

Reverse Engineering: Aquarium Pump

3D Scanning & Parametric Reconstruction

The objective of this project was to reverse engineer a missing lid for an aquarium aerator. The replacement component had to mate accurately with the existing pump housing while allowing the external form to be redesigned rather than simply copied.

01 Data Acquisition

Used an EinScan SP desktop scanner and turntable to capture the pump housing. Scanner calibration and white balancing were completed before acquiring the geometry used to define the replacement interface.

02 Mesh Preparation & Reconstruction

The scan mesh was imported into Fusion 360, repaired and used to generate section sketches at key planes. These profiles provided the dimensional basis for reconstructing the mating footprint and translating the scan into editable CAD geometry.

03 Styling & Manufacture

Rather than reproducing the original lid directly, T-Spline forms were used to create an organic external shell with ventilation features. Six rapid-prototyping iterations were used to refine fit and tolerance until the printed component achieved a reliable snap-fit with the existing housing.
