Aerospace Seat Bracket Optimization

Additive Manufacturing & Generative Design

The aerospace industry requires lightweight, high-complexity components to reduce fuel consumption and emissions. This project sought to replace a traditional, overengineered solid aluminum seat bracket (163.85g) with an optimized 3D-printed alternative.

01 Material Evaluation

Conducted empirical tensile and flexural testing comparing FDM (Markforged Onyx Carbon), SLS (Nylon PA12), and Material Jetting (J850 Tough). Onyx Carbon proved superior with an ultimate tensile strength of 104.02 MPa and flexural modulus of 5.89 GPa.

02 Generative Setup & FEA

Utilized Autodesk Fusion to define preserve geometries (bolt holes) and obstacle regions. Applied a safety factor constraint of 2.0 and loads simulating both upright (150N) and reclined (200N) passenger positions. Executed mesh-independent static stress studies to validate the generated organic form.

03 Results & Sustainability

The final printed Onyx component weighed just 12.3g, achieving a 92.5% reduction compared to the original 168.3g aluminum part. A Granta Eco-Audit projected a 99.2% reduction in lifecycle carbon emissions over a 20-year aircraft lifespan, with physical testing yielding a safety factor of 3.62.

Bespoke VR Headset Architecture

Spatial Computing & Ergonomics

Standard VR head straps frequently fail to accommodate afro-textured hair, causing severe ergonomic discomfort and texture compression. Inspired by the "over the top" design philosophy, this project aimed to create a bespoke, collision-aware VR headset mount tailored for a Pixel 8 Pro device.

01 Dual-Modality Scanning

Utilized an Artec Leo to capture highly accurate rigid skeletal geometry (face and forehead) for precise contact pad data. Simultaneously used Kiri Engine (Photogrammetry) to capture the volumetric boundary of the hair. Both meshes were then aligned and merged within Fusion 360 to create a holistic, collision-aware digital twin.

02 Spatial Ideation & DFA

Initial concepting and spatial layout were conducted in VR using Gravity Sketch to ensure intuitive form finding. Following this, precise 15mm offsets were optically engineered between the user's eyes and the lenses, incorporating specific Design for Assembly (DFA) features for securely mounting the mobile device.

03 Generative Realization

In Fusion 360's generative environment, preserves were set for the lens frames, head pads, and phone clips. The face, visual path, and hair volume acted as strict obstacle geometries. Applying a 5N downward load allowed the AI solver to generate an organic, lightweight cantilever structure that perfectly avoids hair compression.

Bike Curious: L3E Urban Mobility

Rapid Prototyping & Iterative Design

The "Rapid prototyping coursework 25938962.pdf" details the creation of "Bike Curious," an L3E class electric moped designed to bridge the gap between 15.5 mph e-bikes and heavy ICE motorcycles for a 10-mile urban commute[cite: 1]. The development heavily emphasized iterative digital design, troubleshooting, and rapid physical prototyping[cite: 1].

01 AI-Assisted Ideation & CAD

Initial concepts were generated using Vizcom AI with prompts like "A futuristic yet retro inspired light electric motorbike," taking inspiration from the Yamaha SR400[cite: 1]. The AI-generated side profile was imported as a canvas into Autodesk Fusion[cite: 1]. The digital model was constructed using form tools, splines, and extruded cuts, incorporating intricate details like embossed tire marks on the wheels and a custom battery casing engineered with a -0.02mm offset for a precise, snug mechanical fit[cite: 1].

02 Prototyping Failures & Iteration

Early FDM prototyping revealed several structural and mechanical flaws[cite: 1]. Initial prints suffered from severe "spaghettiing" because the nozzle Z-axis was calibrated too high, drastically weakening layer adhesion[cite: 1]. Subsequent print attempts experienced warping and layer shifts, likely caused by the nozzle colliding with the physical print mid-operation[cite: 1]. Additionally, the first completed physical model featured handlebars that were too thin, causing them to break under normal handling, prompting a structural redesign[cite: 1].

03 Print Optimization & Assembly

To resolve persistent printability issues, the CAD model was split into two halves joined by internal pins and pinholes, which completely eliminated the need for major support structures[cite: 1]. For the battery holder overhangs, organic supports were painted on to minimize material waste[cite: 1]. The final model was printed using a 0.6mm nozzle at a 0.3mm layer height, yielding a rapid 3.35-hour production time[cite: 1]. The surface finish was drastically improved by reducing the ironing flow rate from 15% to 10% to prevent over-extrusion, and by slowing external perimeter printing speeds to 20mm/s[cite: 1].

Reverse Engineering: Aquarium Pump

3D Scanning & Parametric Reconstruction

The objective of this project was to reverse engineer a missing lid for an aquarium aerator. This required a precise scan-to-CAD workflow to ensure the replacement part mated perfectly with the existing complex geometry of the pump base.

01 Data Acquisition

Utilized an Einscan SP desktop scanner and turntable. Conducted precise camera calibration and white balancing before capturing the baseline point cloud data of the pump housing to ensure maximum dimensional accuracy.

02 Mesh Prep & Reconstruction

Imported the unwatertight model into Fusion 360. Employed the mesh workspace tools to stitch and repair holes, resulting in a clean base trimesh. Used "Create Mesh Section Sketch" to generate 2D profiles at specific planes, fitting curves to reconstruct the exact mounting footprint required for the lid.

03 Styling & Manufacture

Rather than a direct replica, I utilized T-Spline quadballs to design an organic, "blowfish" inspired aesthetic for the upper shell, complete with ventilation holes. Handled 6 iterations of rapid prototyping to dial in tolerances, resulting in a perfect snap-fit.

Digital Transformation & Strategy

Engineering Management, Ethics, and Systems Thinking

Engineering extends beyond designing physical parts; it requires building the robust frameworks that allow those parts to be manufactured sustainably, profitably, and ethically. This project details a comprehensive management overhaul for an established SME operating on the brink of insolvency due to outdated, reactive protocols.

Lean Six Sigma & IoT

Analyzed £50,000+ per-intervention reactive maintenance costs. Proposed a structural transition using the DMAIC framework to standardize protocols. Architected a system utilizing Industry 4.0 IoT sensors linked to an automated ERP system to trigger Just-In-Time (JIT) logistics and predictive maintenance, cutting operational waste and lead times.

Change Management

Implementing digital workflows in a 30-year legacy business risks severe cultural shock. Applied Kotter’s 8-Step Change Model to navigate internal resistance, upskill legacy employees, and transition operations seamlessly while aligning the new structure with ISO 9001 (Quality) and ISO 14001 (Environmental) standards.

Ethical Procurement & Regulatory Compliance

Alongside operational efficiency, modern engineering management demands rigorous ethical standards. Modeled complex ethical dilemmas occurring in million-pound tendering environments to establish compliant procurement workflows:

  • Anti-Corruption Frameworks: Designed protocols adhering strictly to the UK Bribery Act 2010 (Section 2 & 7), mitigating risks of unmerited financial advantages that endanger contractor safety and corporate liability.
  • Professional Accountability: Embedded requirements aligned with the APM Code of Professional Conduct and UK-SPEC (Engineering Council). Engineered solutions like isolated Common Data Environments (CDE) to eliminate off-channel communications and protect junior engineers from decision fatigue and emotional manipulation during vendor selection.
