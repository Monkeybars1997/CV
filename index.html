Aerospace Seat Bracket Optimization

Additive Manufacturing & Generative Design

The aerospace industry requires lightweight, high-complexity components to reduce fuel consumption and emissions. This project sought to replace a traditional, overengineered solid aluminum seat bracket (163.85g) with an optimized 3D-printed alternative.

Methodology

  • Material Evaluation: Conducted empirical tensile and flexural testing comparing FDM (Markforged Onyx Carbon), SLS (Nylon PA12), and Material Jetting (J850 Tough). Onyx Carbon proved superior with an ultimate tensile strength of 104.02 MPa and flexural modulus of 5.89 GPa.
  • Generative Design: Utilized Autodesk Fusion to define preserve geometries (bolt holes) and obstacle regions. Applied a safety factor constraint of 2 and loads simulating both upright (150N) and reclined (200N) passenger positions.
  • FEA Validation: Executed mesh-independent static stress studies to validate the generated organic form, ensuring sub-5mm maximum displacement constraints were met.

Results & Impact

  • 92.5% Weight Reduction: Final printed Onyx component weighed just 12.3g compared to the original 168.3g aluminum part.
  • Physical Validation: Physical testing yielded a safety factor of 3.62, outperforming digital FEA predictions and ensuring real-world safety.
  • Sustainability: A Granta Eco-Audit projected a 99.2% reduction in lifecycle carbon emissions, fuel costs, and energy consumption over a 20-year aircraft lifespan.

Bespoke VR Headset Architecture

Spatial Computing & Ergonomics

Standard VR head straps frequently fail to accommodate afro-textured hair, causing severe ergonomic discomfort and texture compression. Inspired by the "over the top" design philosophy, this project aimed to create a bespoke, collision-aware VR headset mount tailored for a Pixel 8 Pro device.

The Digital Twin Workflow

  • Dual-Modality Scanning: Utilized an Artec Leo to capture highly accurate rigid skeletal geometry (face and forehead) for precise contact pad data. Simultaneously used Kiri Engine (Photogrammetry) to capture the volumetric boundary of the hair, which structured light typically misses.
  • Data Fusion: Aligned and merged both meshes within Fusion 360 to create a holistic, collision-aware digital twin of the user's head.
  • Spatial Ideation: Initial concepting and spatial layout were conducted in VR using Gravity Sketch.
  • Optical Engineering & DFA: Designed precise 15mm offsets between the user's eyes and the optical lenses. Incorporated specific Design for Assembly (DFA) features for mounting the mobile device.

Generative Realization

In Fusion 360's generative environment, preserves were set for the lens frames, head pads, and phone clips. The face, visual path, and hair volume were set as strict obstacle geometries. Applying a 5N downward load (SF 2.0) allowed the AI solver to generate an organic, lightweight cantilever structure that perfectly avoids hair compression while securely holding the device at the correct focal length.

Bike Curious: L3E Urban Mobility

Sustainable Vehicle Design & Prototyping

Addressing the gap between underpowered e-bikes and heavy ICE motorcycles, "Bike Curious" is a concept for an L3E class electric moped designed for a 10-mile dual-carriageway commute. The design ethos blends a retro cafe racer aesthetic with cutting-edge sustainable materials.

Bio-Based Material Innovation

To ensure exemption from zero-emission zone regulations and achieve true carbon neutrality, the vehicle architecture proposes:

  • Body: Flax fiber composite (100% biodegradable, 85% lower CO2 emissions than carbon fiber).
  • Frame: Hydro Circal 100R Aluminum (100% scrap, 95% less energy to produce).
  • Seat: Mycelium leather (Higher tensile strength than cowhide, fraction of the CO2 footprint).
  • Battery: Lignin anode solid-state battery (Bio-negative byproduct, 3x faster charging).
  • Tires: Russian Dandelion rubber (Decoupling from SE Asian deforestation).

Rapid Prototyping Process

  • Iterative ideation translated from rough concepts to Vizcom AI renders, and finally to Fusion 360 solid modeling.
  • Utilized a Prusa i3 MK3 for scaled prototyping. Early prints failed due to z-axis height and thin features (spaghettiing), which informed design adjustments.
  • Optimized print settings: split models to eliminate supports, utilized organic supports where necessary, and applied top-surface ironing (10% flow rate) for a premium post-processed finish.

Reverse Engineering: Aquarium Pump

3D Scanning & Parametric Reconstruction

The objective of this project was to reverse engineer a missing lid for an aquarium aerator. This required a precise scan-to-CAD workflow to ensure the replacement part mated perfectly with the existing complex geometry of the pump base.

The Workflow

  • Data Acquisition: Utilized an Einscan SP desktop scanner and turntable. Conducted precise camera calibration and white balancing before capturing the baseline point cloud data of the pump housing.
  • Mesh Preparation: Imported the unwatertight model into Fusion 360. Employed the mesh workspace tools to stitch and repair holes, resulting in a clean, workable base trimesh.
  • Surface Reconstruction: Used "Create Mesh Section Sketch" to generate 2D profiles at specific planes. These profiles were then utilized to fit curves, generate parametric geometry, and reconstruct the exact mounting footprint (pegs and cutouts) required for the lid.
  • Design Styling: Rather than a direct replica, I utilized T-Spline quadballs to design an organic, "blowfish" inspired aesthetic for the upper shell, complete with ventilation holes for the pump motor.
  • Manufacture: Handled 6 iterations of rapid prototyping to dial in tolerances. Final part printed using organic supports, resulting in a perfect snap-fit onto the original hardware.

Digital Transformation & Strategy

Engineering Management, Ethics, and Systems Thinking

Engineering extends beyond designing physical parts; it requires building the robust frameworks that allow those parts to be manufactured sustainably, profitably, and ethically. This project details a comprehensive management overhaul for an established SME operating on the brink of insolvency due to outdated, reactive protocols.

Lean Six Sigma & IoT

Analyzed £50,000+ per-intervention reactive maintenance costs. Proposed a structural transition using the DMAIC framework to standardize protocols. Architected a system utilizing Industry 4.0 IoT sensors linked to an automated ERP system to trigger Just-In-Time (JIT) logistics and predictive maintenance, cutting operational waste and lead times.

Change Management

Implementing digital workflows in a 30-year legacy business risks severe cultural shock. Applied Kotter’s 8-Step Change Model to navigate internal resistance, upskill legacy employees, and transition operations seamlessly while aligning the new structure with ISO 9001 (Quality) and ISO 14001 (Environmental) standards.

Ethical Procurement & Regulatory Compliance

Alongside operational efficiency, modern engineering management demands rigorous ethical standards. Modeled complex ethical dilemmas occurring in million-pound tendering environments to establish compliant procurement workflows:

  • Anti-Corruption Frameworks: Designed protocols adhering strictly to the UK Bribery Act 2010 (Section 2 & 7), mitigating risks of unmerited financial advantages that endanger contractor safety and corporate liability.
  • Professional Accountability: Embedded requirements aligned with the APM Code of Professional Conduct and UK-SPEC (Engineering Council). Engineered solutions like isolated Common Data Environments (CDE) to eliminate off-channel communications and protect junior engineers from decision fatigue and emotional manipulation during vendor selection.
